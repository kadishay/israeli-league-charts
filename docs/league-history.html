The Israeli league pyramid, 1928–2026

Israel's football pyramid has been rebuilt eight times and has lost ten championships to war, boycott and factional walkout. Four more seasons were played as parallel district leagues with no national table at all. All of that has to be encoded in the data before a single club's chart can be drawn.

The league of the Mandate era was the Eretz Israel League (ליגת ארץ ישראל), run from 1928 to 1948 by the Eretz Israel Football Association and treated as the direct ancestor of today's Ligat ha'Al. English Wikipedia files it under the title "Palestine League"; the native name is the one used here.

The association was founded in August 1928, and the IFA still records a 1928 title for Maccabi Hasmonean Jerusalem — but no season was played that year. The entry comes from the wording of the association's FIFA admission notice, is recognised as erroneous, and is generally put down to public relations. The first attempt at a real league, 1929/30, was stopped by the 1929 riots; the second, from 3 October 1930, collapsed within weeks. The first championship anyone finished was 1931/32, won by British Police — a team of Mandate policemen. The first title of the independent era was 1949/50 (Maccabi Tel Aviv).

So the x-axis runs from 1929/30, and it has to be indexed by season name rather than by year: the league moved to calendar-year seasons between 1937 and 1940, so 1935/36 is followed directly by 1937 — there is no 1936/37 — and 1966/68 is a single two-year championship, so there is no 1967/68.

What level existed when

Read down a column for the pyramid in that era; read across a row for one league's journey. Every insertion at the top pushed everything below it down a level, which is why tier two means Liga Alef in 1960, Liga Artzit in 1980 and Liga Leumit today. This is the single most important thing for the chart: a club's tier is not a property of its league name.

Liga Dalet is the awkward one: it ran for the single season 1958/59 at tier five, vanished, returned in 1969/70 at tier five, was pushed to tier six by the arrival of Liga Artzit in 1976, and was scrapped for good in 1985 as club numbers shrank. The second tier of the Mandate and early-state years went by several names for the same thing — Machleka Bet, then the district leagues (ליגות מחוזיות), then Liga Bet — running 1930/31, then 1937 through 1954/55. There was no third tier before 1951, simply for want of clubs.

One cell still resists clean sourcing: the top flight in 1954/55. RSSSF heads its final table "Liga Leumit", while Wikipedia keeps Liga Alef on top until 1955/56. It is tier one under either name, so the chart is unaffected — but the era boundary in the data follows RSSSF, since that is where the standings come from.

Tiers three and below are regional, and have been since Liga Alef split north/south in 1963/64 and Liga Bet split four ways in 1959/60. A club in Liga Alef finishes fifth in its region, not fifth in the country — so below tier two a single national ranking does not exist, and the chart has to place those seasons in a band rather than at an exact height.

Seasons with no champion

Ten seasons produced no title, eight of them in the Mandate era, when the league was repeatedly wrecked by political violence and by the feud between the Maccabi and Hapoel sports federations. The wars that did not stop the league

Worth stating because it is the obvious assumption to make: the Six-Day War, the Yom Kippur War and the war beginning in October 2023 all interrupted fixtures but none cost a championship. 1973/74 was completed (Maccabi Netanya), and 2023/24 was suspended in October and resumed (Maccabi Tel Aviv). The Six-Day War falls inside the deliberate two-year 1966/68 season, which is a scheduling decision rather than a cancellation. Every season from 1955/56 onward has a champion.

The district-league seasons

Four seasons sit in their own category: played, with a recognised champion, but with no national table. The Arab revolt and then the war made it impossible for clubs to host sides from other districts, so the top flight was broken into regional divisions — in 1939 the top two tiers were merged and redealt into seven of them. The champion is whoever won the strongest district.

Why the title counts don't agree

1939 is the test case. The IFA recognised the title until 2003, then dropped it on the grounds that no official national senior league had taken place. Maccabi Tel Aviv went on claiming it regardless, and after an opinion from Prof. Yair Galili, head of the association's research unit, the IFA restored recognition on 26 May 2024 — the "fifth star" decision.

The same irregularity leaves English and Hebrew Wikipedia disagreeing about 1934/35 and 1938. Hebrew and the IFA credit Hapoel Tel Aviv with both, giving them five Mandate-era titles; English Wikipedia marks both unfinished and counts three, stating outright that "controversy remains concerning the number of titles won by Hapoel Tel Aviv". This document follows the Hebrew navbox, which strikes through neither, so both count as played. Anything reporting title counts should say which convention it is using.

Consequences for the chart

Five structural facts have to be modelled rather than assumed, or the chart will be quietly wrong:

  1. Tier is derived, not stored. Keep (season, league name, position) and resolve the tier from the era table above at render time.
  2. The x-axis is a list of season names, not a range of years. There is no 1936/37 and no 1967/68, and 1937–1940 are single-year seasons sitting between split-year ones. Generating columns by looping over years will silently invent two seasons and misalign everything after 1936.
  3. The y-axis rescales every few seasons. Cumulative rank across the pyramid depends on the size of every tier above, and the top flight alone has swung between 10 and 18 clubs.
  4. Ten columns carry no position at all and must never be interpolated across. They need their own visual treatment — hatching, as on the English charts.
  5. Four more columns have a champion but no ranking, and neither do any seasons below tier two once the lower divisions went regional. Both cases can only be placed within a band, and they deserve a different mark from the ten empty columns: "played, position unknowable" is not "not played".
